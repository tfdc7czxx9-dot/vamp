# IP Guidelines: Public-Domain Sources

> Owner: `legal-compliance-specialist` with `creative-director`.
> This is a working guide, **not legal advice**. Re-verify before announcing the
> game and before each launch. Public-domain status differs by country. In the
> EU, works are generally free 70 years after the author's death; in the US it
> depends on the publication date.

## Free to use (source texts)

| Author | Died | Status (EU / US) | Usable works (examples) |
|--------|------|------------------|-------------------------|
| Jules Verne | 1905 | PD / PD | *Journey to the Centre of the Earth*, *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas*, *From the Earth to the Moon*, *The Mysterious Island*, *Robur the Conqueror*, *Around the World in 80 Days* |
| H. G. Wells | 1946 | PD (since 2017) / PD for works published before the US cutoff (all listed here) | *The Time Machine*, *The War of the Worlds*, *The First Men in the Moon*, *The Island of Doctor Moreau*, *The Invisible Man* |
| Arthur Conan Doyle | 1930 | PD / PD (all stories) | *The Lost World* (possible DLC world) |
| Georges Méliès | 1938 | PD / PD | Film titles and imagery of *Le Voyage dans la Lune*, *Le Voyage à travers l'impossible* |

Only the **original texts and the original illustrations of their time** are free.
For example, the engravings by Riou and de Neuville in the Hetzel editions are PD.

## Do NOT use (protected later adaptations)

- **Disney's *20,000 Leagues Under the Sea* (1954)**: its Nautilus design (riveted, crocodile-like hull, large round window). Design our own Nautilus-inspired vessel from the novel's description.
- ***The War of the Worlds* adaptations**, e.g. the 2005 film, Jeff Wayne's musical and the TV series: their tripod designs, sounds ("ulla") and music. Design from Wells' text and the 1906 Alvim Corréa illustrations (PD).
- **Film versions of *The Time Machine*** (1960, 2002): their Morlock and Eloi designs
- ***The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen*** (Alan Moore comic, film): do not build a "team of famous Victorian literary heroes" premise or style around it. Our leaders are original characters.
- **Edgar Rice Burroughs worlds** (Barsoom, Pellucidar, Tarzan): trademarks are actively enforced. Avoid entirely.
- **"HD-2D"** is a Square Enix trademark: never use the term for our game, in store copy, tags or press. Say "2D-3D diorama" or "pixel art in 3D worlds".
- **Final Fantasy / Octopath / other JRPGs**: the visual *technique* and the orchestral *genre* are free to use. Their names, logos, characters, sprites, UI layouts and **melodies/motifs** (preludes, fanfares, battle themes) are not. Never write "like Final Fantasy" in store copy (store policies on misleading metadata + trademark).
- Any modern game, film or comic in the genre: no copying of names, character designs, UI or music.

## How we use the sources

- Worlds, creatures and historical vehicles serve as **inspiration and setting**. We do not retell the plots.
- PD characters such as Captain Nemo or Professor Lidenbrock may appear as cameos or unlockables. The **main cast is original** so the franchise remains our own IP.
- Credit the sources in the credits ("Inspired by the novels of Jules Verne and H. G. Wells").
- Run a trademark search on the final title and the main character names before the Steam "Coming Soon" page goes live.
